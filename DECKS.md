# deck directions

we picked playful group-chat habits for the default deck and gave the written rounds their own deck, Spotlight. each deck has five rounds. the directions below are a backlog of prompts and packs to playtest.

## 1. group chat

light teasing about habits the group recognizes. this is the clearest first deck: people can answer from what they already know about each other.

sample cards:

- pick: who can revive a dead group chat with one message?
- pick: who says “omw” before they have left the house?
- write: what would {player}'s unofficial role in this group be?

## 2. soft launch

affectionate, specific observations. good for close friends, or for ending a session on a warmer note. it still needs a real detail, or it starts sounding like a compliment card.

sample cards:

- pick: who notices when someone's having a quieter day?
- pick: who makes a new person feel included without making a big deal of it?
- write: what small habit makes {player} easy to be around?

## 3. harmless hills

low-stakes opinions and choices that invite people to defend their picks. try it with a group that likes friendly disagreement, but keep the subject silly enough that a vote doesn't feel like a verdict on someone's character.

sample cards:

- pick: who would order their usual at a restaurant with a menu full of new things?
- pick: who would take the longer route for a better snack stop?
- write: what harmless hill would {player} defend with suspicious confidence?

## 4. the receipts

prompts about real moments the group still brings up. this should produce stories, but it depends on shared history, so it may leave newer groups with nothing to say.

sample cards:

- pick: who remembers the exact details whenever an old group story comes up?
- pick: who could turn a small mishap into the story everyone retells?
- write: what's a moment when {player} accidentally became the main character?

## 5. chaos mode

absurd hypotheticals with no backstory required. easy to jump into with a mixed group, though the current “who would...” style can blur into group chat after a few rounds.

sample cards:

- pick: who could get elected mayor by accident while asking for directions?
- pick: who would join a parade for one block and finish the whole route?
- write: what ridiculous thing would {player} put on a billboard?

## what i'd test first

run Group Chat with two groups and Spotlight with one close group. see whether people can get into the vote rounds right away and whether the written answers sound like something only that group could have come up with. if those land, Harmless Hills and The Receipts are the next two directions worth comparing.

## what to watch

for each prompt, note whether votes spread across several people or keep landing on the same person across groups. after the reveal, listen for an immediate reason, laugh, or story. for write cards, check whether answers are specific to the named player and easy to share aloud. confusion, repeated passes, answers that could fit anyone, or an awkward silence are good reasons to rewrite a card.

## two questions

1. should the first decks work for friends who met tonight, or reward groups with years of shared stories?
2. when a Spotlight answer gets a laugh, does the group want to see who wrote it or keep guessing?
